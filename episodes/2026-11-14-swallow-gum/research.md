# Research — What Happens If You Swallow Chewing Gum? (2026-11-14)

## Core findings (all dated, verified sources)

1. **Gum base is indigestible synthetic rubber — but it doesn't stay stuck.** It is moved through the
   digestive tract by normal gut motion and passed in stool, usually within a few days.
   Source: Mayo Clinic expert answer, mayoclinic.org (accessed 2023).
2. **The "7 years in your stomach" claim is false.** Mayo Clinic explicitly debunks the myth.
3. **Chronic daily swallowing is the real risk.** Milov et al., Pediatrics 1998;102(2):e22 (PMID 9685468):
   children who swallowed several pieces a day (one case combined with coins) developed gum bezoars —
   hardened masses causing gastrointestinal blockage; one 4-year-old required surgical removal.
4. **Acute obstruction case.** Sinopidis et al., J Paediatr Child Health 2021;57(9):1533-1535 (PMID 33373485):
   gum bezoar causing acute intestinal obstruction in a child.

## Editorial framing

- One swallow: harmless, out in days ("OUT IN DAYS ✓" pill).
- 7-years claim: myth ("7 YEARS ✗ / JUST A MYTH" pill).
- Habit swallowing: documented bezoar cases in children, surgery claim scoped to reported child cases.
